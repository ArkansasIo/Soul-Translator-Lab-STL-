REFERENCE LIBRARY

Research notes

Introductory context for neural systems, BCIs, measurement, and responsible research.

01 / SYSTEMS

Brain systems

Complex brain functions arise from interacting networks. No single structure is a complete memory store or a consciousness switch.

  • Cerebral cortexDistributed perception, language, planning and action.
  • Hippocampal formationImportant in many forms of episodic and spatial memory.
  • ThalamusParticipates in relaying and coordinating information streams.
  • CerebellumSupports timing, coordination, and motor learning.
02 / METHODS

Measurement & BCIs

Brain-computer interfaces decode limited, task-specific information; results depend on sensors, training, and context.

  • EEGScalp electrical potentials, high temporal resolution and limited spatial precision.
  • fMRIIndirect blood-oxygenation signals with slower temporal response.
  • Signal processingFiltering and statistical analysis require awareness of noise and artifacts.
03 / LIMITATIONS

What this project is not

The “Soul Translator” is a fictional concept. Current science does not offer technology to upload a person or copy unrestricted mental contents.

  • No real neural inputAll values in Signal Studio are generated synthetic samples.
  • No consciousness transferAgent state is a small, inspectable data structure.
  • No clinical useThis demo is not a medical device or diagnostic tool.
04 / ETHICS

Responsible research

Neural data can be sensitive. Human-subject research requires appropriate expert oversight, consent, protection, and compliance.

  • ConsentUse informed, voluntary participation for human research.
  • Data minimizationCollect only what is needed and define retention.
  • SecurityRestrict access and avoid publishing sensitive raw data.
