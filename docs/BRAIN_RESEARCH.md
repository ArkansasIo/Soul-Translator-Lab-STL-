# Human Brain Research Notes

## Scope
Educational starting point, not a clinical reference. The brain is an interconnected network; functions emerge from interactions among many structures and the body.

## Organization
Commonly discussed systems include cerebral cortex, hippocampal formation, thalamus, brainstem, basal ganglia, cerebellum, and amygdala-related circuits. These are not independent modules.

## Neurons and communication
Neurons integrate inputs and communicate via electrical changes in membrane potential and chemical synaptic signaling. Glial cells also contribute to neural function, metabolic support, immune responses, and regulation of the neural environment.

## Memory
Working memory, episodic memory, semantic knowledge, habits, and motor learning involve partially distinct but interacting systems. Encoding, consolidation, and retrieval depend on attention, sleep, context, and prior knowledge. Remembering is often reconstructive.

## Consciousness
Researchers distinguish level of consciousness, such as wakefulness, from conscious contents. Neuroscience investigates neural correlates and mechanisms, but no single theory explains every aspect of subjective experience. Intelligent behavior alone does not prove subjective experience.

## Measuring brain activity
- EEG records scalp voltage differences; it is temporally precise but spatially limited.
- fMRI measures blood-oxygenation-related changes indirectly and relatively slowly.
- Intracranial recordings provide local signals in specific clinical/research contexts but are invasive.
- BCIs map measured signals to constrained outputs; they do not provide general access to thoughts, memories, or identity.

## Why a real STL is not currently feasible
No demonstrated technology can scan and reproduce the full state of a living brain, transfer a person's consciousness into a computer, or guarantee preservation of personal identity in a digital copy.
